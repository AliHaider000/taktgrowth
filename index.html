Chapter I · Four days in Sofia

The stand starts the conversation. Your website decides who gets the quote.

This week, more than sixteen thousand people walk through five halls at Inter Expo Center. You shake hands, swap cards, and promise to send a quote. On Friday the stand comes down. On Monday the real question starts: which of those conversations becomes an order?

Most of the decision happens after the expo, at a desk. The engineer you met goes back to the office and checks you out. They read your website, compare you with two or three others, and decide who gets the RFQ. By the time they call, most of the choice is made.

That is not a feeling. It is what engineers say when they are asked.

Chapter II · The gap

Half of Bulgarian manufacturers are invisible online. That is your opening.

On Wednesday the machine-building chamber hosts a panel on the industry's competitiveness. Here is a number that belongs in that room. Bulgaria has the lowest share of companies with a website in the European Union. Among manufacturers, just over half have one. Only a third have one in two or more languages.

If your buyers sit in Stuttgart, Vienna or Bucharest, they search in their own language. They find whoever answered them in it.

The upside is simple. In most Bulgarian industrial niches, page one is held by a handful of thin catalogue sites. You do not have to beat fifty strong competitors. You have to be clearer than five weak ones.

Chapter III · Who is in the halls

205 exhibitors, three kinds of company, one shared problem.

I went through all 205 companies in this year's catalogue. About half are Bulgarian dealers and local offices of foreign brands: Mazak, Okuma, KUKA, Fanuc, Fronius, Iscar. About 25 are Bulgarian makers and integrators. The rest are foreign manufacturers, from a full Czech pavilion to laser makers from China who now exhibit direct.

They sell very different things. They lose leads in the same place: between the handshake and the RFQ.

The dealersells a foreign brand in Bulgaria
"The buyer found the machine on the maker's site. We only get the call to quote a price."
  • Win the local searches: brand plus Bulgaria, service, spare parts, financing
  • Show your principal how many leads the territory produced
  • Stand out from makers who now sell direct
What fixes it: brand and service pages, lead tracking, reports your principal can read
The makermachining, fabrication, hydraulics
"We have the machines and the tolerances. German buyers just never find us."
  • Capability pages: machine list, materials, tolerances, certificates
  • English and German versions that rank where the buyers are
  • An RFQ form that takes a drawing, not just a name
What fixes it: a rebuilt site, export SEO, LinkedIn, follow-up email
The integratorrobotics, automation, industrial software
"Our sale takes nine months, and the global brands own every search we care about."
  • Application pages and case studies for each industry you serve
  • Demo bookings tracked to the deal
  • Being named when buyers ask an AI assistant for a shortlist
What fixes it: content, AI search visibility, LinkedIn, nurture email

Notice what is missing from that list: slogans. Engineers want specs as text, real lead times, drawings and proof. Most Bulgarian industrial websites bury that in a PDF, or leave it out.

Engineers trust tolerances, not adjectives. Put the specs on the page.
